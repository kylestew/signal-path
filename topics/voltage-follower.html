Voltage Follower

Same voltage out, but better able to drive the next stage

What It Does

A voltage follower copies the input voltage to the output without trying to make it bigger. Its job is buffering: the source sees an easy load, and the next stage gets a stronger driver.

Why It Matters

  • Stops the next stage from loading down the source
  • Keeps one part of the circuit from interfering with another
  • Lets weak or sensitive sources drive cables and downstream inputs more cleanly

The Circuit

Input goes to the non-inverting (+) pin. Output connects directly back to the inverting (−) pin — 100% negative feedback. Output tracks input. The op-amp still needs power rails even though they are not shown in this simple drawing.

Buffer vs Follower

In everyday use, "voltage follower" and "buffer" usually mean the same thing. "Follower" describes the behavior (output follows input). "Buffer" describes the purpose (isolate stages). An emitter follower or source follower can do a similar buffering job, but it does not copy the voltage as exactly as an op-amp follower.

When You Need One

  • Before a long cable run
  • After a potentiometer
  • Between a high-impedance source and a lower-impedance input
  • At a module output that needs to drive the next module cleanly

Plain English: same voltage, less interaction, fewer loading problems.
