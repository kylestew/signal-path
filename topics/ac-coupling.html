AC Coupling

Block DC, pass AC — the most common filter in audio circuits

What It Does

A capacitor in series with the signal path blocks any DC component and passes AC through. The capacitor charges up to the DC level, then only the changing part of the signal appears on the other side.

Why It Matters

  • Removes DC offset between circuit stages so each stage can set its own bias point
  • Protects downstream inputs from unexpected DC voltages
  • Lets you chain modules without worrying about DC accumulation

The Circuit

A series capacitor and a resistor to ground form a high-pass filter. Signals above the cutoff frequency pass through; signals below get attenuated. Adjust the component values and watch the output change.

Cutoff Frequency

The cutoff frequency is the point where the output drops to 70.7% (−3dB) of the input:

fc = 1 / (2πRC)

Below cutoff, the signal attenuates at 20dB per decade. Above cutoff, everything passes through at full amplitude.

Rules of Thumb

  • 1μF + 10kΩ → fc ≈ 16 Hz — good general-purpose audio coupling
  • 100nF + 10kΩ → fc ≈ 160 Hz — passes mid and high frequencies
  • 10μF + 1kΩ → fc ≈ 16 Hz — same cutoff, lower impedance path

For eurorack audio signals, aim for a cutoff well below 20 Hz so you don't lose any audible content. 1μF into a typical 100kΩ input impedance gives fc ≈ 1.6 Hz — plenty of margin.
